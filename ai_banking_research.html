      What went wrong
        Lessons
          Cross-cutting lessons learned
          What works
          Narrow, well-defined use cases (document parsing, anomaly detection) consistently outperform broad "general AI" deployments. Start with a specific task with measurable output.
          What works
          Human-in-the-loop design matters. JPMorgan's COiN flags clauses for lawyers to review — it doesn't replace them. This framing accelerated regulatory approval and user trust.
          What works
          Data quality is the primary success driver. Fraud detection improvements at Mastercard came after years of clean, labeled transaction data — not after model improvements alone.
          What fails
          Algorithmic bias in credit models carries serious legal and reputational risk. Models trained on historical data embed historical discrimination. Ongoing auditing and explainability are not optional in regulated environments.
          What fails
          Overpromising AI capabilities to leadership before production validation leads to over-investment and credibility loss when results underwhelm (Watson, early roboadvisor pilots).
          What fails
          Chatbots without genuine NLU degrade CX. Customers quickly learn to ask for a human agent, making the bot a friction layer rather than a resolution layer.
