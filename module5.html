01 Key Lessons
01
The Core Insight
Analytics does not fail because the math is wrong

It fails because the organisation was not ready to use it. The data science is rarely the bottleneck. The five organisational conditions — DELTA — almost always are.

02
Leadership
Leadership is the DELTA multiplier

Without someone at VP or ED level who actively commissions, challenges, and acts on analytical work, the other four DELTA conditions — data, enterprise, targets, analysts — stall. The analytics sits in a warehouse unused.

03
BCG Framework
Two questions determine whether to act on any finding

How confident are you in the relationship — based on frequency and clarity of causality? And do the benefits of acting outweigh the cost of being wrong? These two questions apply regardless of domain or data type.

04
OCBC — Operational
A capability that exists but isn't prioritised is not a capability

OCBC's behavioural analytics infrastructure partially existed before the scam crisis. What was missing was leadership mandate. The crisis and MAS regulatory pressure changed the risk/reward calculus — and the capability was deployed within weeks.

05
Standard Chartered — Marketing
Deploying a tool and getting people to use it are different problems

The Next Best Conversation analytics was strong from day one. RM adoption was the bottleneck. The reframe from "the system replaces your judgment" to "the system gives you ammunition" was what unlocked usage — a management and communication challenge, not a technical one.

06
Mizuho — Financial
Precisely defined targets unlock analytical value

Mizuho's SME credit initiative succeeded because the problem was stated precisely: assessment takes too long and excludes creditworthy borrowers. That specificity gave the analytics team clear success criteria. Vague mandates produce vague analytics.

07
Management Responsibility
Most analytics failures are management failures

Not statistical errors. Someone did not ask the right question upfront. Someone accepted a convenient finding without pushing back. Someone did not create the conditions for an analyst to say "this data is not clean enough." These are all management decisions.

08
The Two-Step Test
DELTA checks the input. BCG checks the output.

Use DELTA to assess whether your organisation produced a trustworthy finding. Use the BCG matrix to decide what to do with it. Both checks are needed. Passing one and skipping the other is where expensive mistakes happen.

Institution Domain The Finding Key DELTA Condition BCG Quadrant
OCBC Bank
Singapore · 2021–23 		Operational Behavioural baseline analytics detects fraud patterns in real time. SGD 660M industry losses in 2022 fell 42% by 2023. Leadership. The capability existed. The mandate did not. A public crisis and MAS regulation changed the risk/reward calculus overnight. Top right — high confidence, high reward. Act.
Standard Chartered
Asia Pacific · 2020–23 		Marketing Clients contacted with contextually relevant, data-triggered conversations show materially higher product uptake and lower attrition. Analysts. Not the model quality — the human adoption problem. Senior RMs resisted until framing shifted from "replacement" to "ammunition." Upper mid-right for tool deployment. Higher-stakes actions (RM incentive changes) require moving further up the confidence axis first.
Mizuho Financial Group
Japan / APAC · 2020–23 		Financial Alternative data (transaction history, supply chain, payment behaviour) enables SME credit assessment in days vs. weeks, expanding the addressable lending population. Targets. A precisely defined problem — too slow, too narrow — gave the analytics team clear success criteria. Governance protocols for model-override decisions were equally critical. Centre — medium-high confidence, high stakes. Plot shifts right as model matures. Governance guardrails required to act responsibly in the interim.
Common Thread
Across All Three Cases

In every case, the analytics was not the hardest part. The organisational conditions — mandate, adoption, problem definition — were where the real challenge sat.

All three cases were addressable by managers. None required a data scientist to solve.

03 Frameworks Reference
Framework 1 — DELTA Model · Davenport, Harvard Business School
The five organisational conditions that determine whether analytics produces business value.
Use to diagnose why analytics succeeded or failed. Use to identify where your management effort should focus.
D
Data
Clean, accessible, trusted data the organisation agrees on
E
Enterprise
Analytics capability shared across teams, not siloed in one group
L
Leadership
Senior managers who commission, challenge, and act on data work
T
Targets
Specific measurable business problems analytics is aimed at solving
A
Analysts
People with technical capability AND business judgment
Framework 2 — BCG Correlation Action Matrix · David Ritter, Boston Consulting Group
Two questions determine whether to act on a data finding: (1) How confident are you in the relationship — frequency plus clarity of causality? (2) Do the benefits of acting outweigh the cost of being wrong?
Use to evaluate a specific data finding and decide whether and how to act on it. Apply after the DELTA check confirms the finding is trustworthy.
The Manager's Two-Step Decision Test
Step 1 — DELTA check: Is the data clean? Was the right question asked? Do I trust the people who produced this analysis?
Step 2 — BCG check: How confident am I in this finding? And does acting on it make sense given the stakes?
BCG Matrix
Four Quadrants
High conf · High rewardAct
High conf · Low rewardConsider carefully
Low conf · High rewardLow-risk actions only
Low conf · Low rewardDon't act
Key Principle
Causality is a dial, not a switch

Investing in understanding why a correlation exists raises your position on the confidence axis — which can unlock actions that were previously too risky to take.

Your Takeaway Framework

How was this data sourced, and do I trust it?
DELTA: Data condition
02
Was the right business question asked before the analysis began?
DELTA: Targets condition
03
How confident are we in this finding — and what is the cost if we act and we are wrong?

One Action · Five Working Days

Based on your DELTA self-assessment, identify one specific action to take this week — not a project, not a strategy. One thing.

Weak on Data
Identify one report your team uses regularly where you genuinely do not know how the underlying data is sourced or validated. Ask that question this week.
Weak on Targets
Identify one decision your team currently makes on instinct that could be sharpened with data. Frame it as a question and give it to your analyst.
Weak on Leadership
Find one moment in a meeting this week where data is presented. Push back with the BCG two questions instead of accepting the finding at face value.
Closing Statement · Module 5
"The organisations that win with data are not the ones with the best algorithms. They are the ones with the managers who know how to ask the right question, create the right conditions, and make the right call when the analysis lands on their desk."
