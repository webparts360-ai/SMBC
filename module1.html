Introduction to
Data Analytics

What data analytics is, why it matters at the leadership level, and the manager's framework for interrogating any analysis placed in front of you.

Duration 60 minutes
Format Virtual · Webex
Engagement 5 Mentimeter polls
Assessment 5-question quiz
Day 1 Understanding the Importance of Data Analytics

Learning Outcomes

1

Articulate what data analytics is and why it matters at the managerial level — not the technical level.

2

Distinguish between descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive analytics using banking examples.

3

Apply the three-question manager's framework to any model output or report.

4

Recognise analytics-literate leadership as a competitive and regulatory imperative in APAC banking.

Session Flow

0:00 – 0:10
Opening & Context Setting
Mentimeter word cloud surfaces how participants currently use data. The Waze story introduces data as an upstream decision resource. Frame: you already have data — the question is whether you are using it well.
0:10 – 0:25
The Four Types of Analytics
Descriptive → Diagnostic → Predictive → Prescriptive. Each type explained with a banking use case. Webex breakout: which type does your team rely on most, and which do you wish you had more of?
0:25 – 0:40
The Manager's Three-Question Framework
Before acting on any analysis: (1) What data went in? (2) What assumptions does the model make? (3) What decision does the output support? Mentimeter scale poll on how consistently participants apply this.
0:40 – 0:50
Analytics-Literate Leadership — The TD Bank Case
TD Bank's CEO required senior managers to understand the assumptions behind every financial product the bank depended on. The result: TD identified and exited the riskiest structured products before the 2008 crisis. The contrast with a bank that lost billions because a quant's warning was not understood.
0:50 – 0:60
Reflection & Quiz
Mentimeter closing prompt: "If a new analyst joined your team tomorrow, what would they find?" Five-question Microsoft Forms consolidation quiz before the break.

The Four Types of Analytics

What happened?
Descriptive
Portfolio NPL dashboard. Monthly P&L by segment. Branch performance KPIs. The foundation — most organisations are strong here.
Why did it happen?
Diagnostic
Attribution analysis on NPL deterioration: is it vintage, product, geography, or RMs? This is where most organisations have the largest gap.
What is likely to happen?
Predictive
Credit scoring. Probability of default models. Customer churn prediction. You are already consuming predictive outputs — the question is whether you interrogate the assumptions behind them.
What should we do?
Prescriptive
Models that not only predict which customers are likely to churn but recommend the intervention most likely to retain them. Where AI and machine learning play an increasing role.

The Manager's Framework

Three Questions — Before Acting on Any Analysis
1. What data went in?  ·  2. What assumptions does the model make?  ·  3. What decision does this output support?
Apply these whether you are reviewing a credit model, a customer attrition dashboard, or a regulatory capital calculation. If your team cannot answer all three in under two minutes, the analysis is not ready for a decision.
Why Question 2 is the hardest

Most managers ask questions 1 and 3 — they check the source and focus on the output. Question 2 requires pushing back on the methodology. It is the question that separates analytics-literate leadership from analytics-consuming leadership. And it is the question most likely to catch a problem before it becomes a decision.

Mentimeter Polls — Five Touchpoints

1
Word Cloud · 0:02
"In one or two words — how does your team currently use data to make decisions?"
Opens the room. Surfaces existing reality before any content is delivered. Results become the opening observation.
2
Multiple Choice · 0:08
"Which best describes how data is typically used in your most important decisions?"
Plants the upstream vs downstream mirror. Seeds the Module 2 theme without the confrontation. Options: data arrives before / alongside / after the decision, or it depends on who is in the room.
3
Multiple Select · 0:20
"Which types of analytics does your team currently rely on? Select all that apply."
Capability map across the four types. Results almost always show descriptive as dominant. Provides visual proof of the diagnostic and predictive gap.
4
Anchored Scale 1–5 · 0:35
"In a situation like [NPL scenario] — how many of the three questions do you typically ask before responding?"
Personal accountability. Preceded by a scenario slide (NPL rises 40bps, recommendation to tighten criteria). Scale anchored with behavioural descriptions at each level. Anonymous. Creates a personal baseline score to revisit at Day 2 close.
5
Open Text · 0:50
"In my team, data could be used more effectively to… (complete the sentence)"
Shifts register from diagnosis to aspiration. Generates material the facilitator can reference throughout the day. Bridges into the TD Bank case and the module close.

Consolidation Quiz — Microsoft Forms

Q1 · NPL ratios are reported by segment and vintage — but no one explains what caused the rise. Which analytics type is missing?
  • A. Descriptive analytics
  • B. Diagnostic analytics
  • C. Predictive analytics
  • D. Prescriptive analytics
Q2 · A model shows 8.3% default probability for a new segment. Which question is most commonly skipped before acting on it?
  • A. What does 8.3% mean for expected loss?
  • B. How does 8.3% compare to our portfolio average?
  • C. What assumptions does this model make about the segment?
  • D. What data went in and how recent is it?
Q3 · What does the Waze story illustrate about how banks should use data?
  • A. Banks need real-time systems like Waze.
  • B. Data should shape decisions before they are made — not confirm them after.
  • C. Banks have more data than Waze and a structural advantage.
  • D. AI can replace human judgment in high-stakes decisions.
Q4 · A new analyst asks: "How do we decide what data to use before making a recommendation?" What is the most mature answer?
  • A. We use the standard monthly dashboard.
  • B. We start with three questions: what data exists, what assumptions, what decision.
  • C. That is a question for the analytics team.
  • D. We look at the most recent report and go from there.
Q5 · TD Bank's advantage in 2008 came primarily from:
  • A. More sophisticated modelling tools than competitors.
  • B. Senior managers who understood product assumptions and could act on the data.
  • C. Its risk team correctly predicting the housing price decline.
  • D. Higher investment in its analytics function than peers.

Key Takeaways

1
Analytics has four types — and most organisations are strong in only two
Descriptive and diagnostic are table stakes. The value gap is predictive and prescriptive. Know where your team sits.
2
Your role is intelligent consumer — not builder
The three-question framework is your tool. It applies whether you are reviewing a credit model, a compliance dashboard, or a regulatory capital submission.
3
Data upstream of the decision is the Waze principle
Most banks use data to report on decisions already made. The competitive advantage is in using it before the decision, not after.
4
Analytics literacy at the leadership level is a regulatory imperative
MAS, HKMA, and BSP are increasing scrutiny of model risk governance. If your team presents a model to a regulator and you cannot speak to its assumptions, that is an institutional risk — not just a technical one.
