Module Day 1 · Session 1
Descriptive Analytics · Type 1
"What is true right now?"

Descriptive analytics answers the most fundamental question: what does the data show? It captures the current state — balances, volumes, ratios, rates — giving managers a reliable snapshot to work from. Without it, everything else fails. With only it, you know what happened, but not why, what comes next, or what to do.

🏦
SME Lending
Portfolio visibility — what does the book look like today?
Portfolio Level
Outstanding loan balances
Total exposure by segment (SME tier, sector), by geography (SG, MY, PH, HK), and by relationship manager. Updated daily.
Credit Quality
NPL ratio — current and 3-month trend
Non-performing loan ratio as a percentage of total outstanding. Trend line shows direction but does not yet explain drivers.
Operations
Approval & disbursement TAT
Average turnaround time from application receipt to disbursement, broken down by channel, product type, and RM tier. Current: [X] days.
Pipeline
New application volumes
Week-on-week pipeline view: applications received, under review, approved, declined, withdrawn. Baseline for capacity planning.
Utilisation
Facility utilisation rates
For revolving credit facilities: how much of the approved limit is being drawn. Segment average and per-account distribution.
Revenue
Net interest income by segment
NII contribution broken down by sector and product type. Compares actual vs plan. Does not explain variance — that is Diagnostic work.
The descriptive limit
When the NPL ratio rises 0.4%, descriptive analytics tells you it happened. It does not tell you whether this is concentrated in one sector, one RM, or spread across the portfolio — or whether it is likely to continue.
Knowing the number is the starting point. It is not the decision.
👥
Retail Banking & CX
Customer base visibility — who do we have, and are they active?
Customer Base
Active customer count — monthly trend
Number of customers with at least one active product, tracked monthly. Trend line shows whether the base is growing, stable, or shrinking.
Product Depth
Products per customer (breadth)
Average number of product holdings per active customer, segmented by relationship tier. Single-product customers are flagged as attrition risk.
Digital
Login frequency by channel
Monthly active users on mobile and internet banking. Feature adoption rates. Drop in login frequency precedes attrition but does not explain it.
Acquisition
Onboarding funnel conversion
Step-by-step drop-off rates across the digital onboarding flow. Shows where customers are leaving — not yet why.
Revenue
Fee income & NII by segment
Contribution by product and customer tier. Monthly vs prior year. A fall in fee income per customer is visible here; its cause is not.
Dormancy
Dormant account report
Accounts with no transaction activity for 90+ days, segmented by product and tenure. Early-stage churn signal — descriptive, not predictive.
⚙️
Digital Journey & Operations
Process visibility — are we meeting service levels today?
Funnel
Step-by-step conversion rates
Percentage of users completing each stage of a digital journey — application, verification, product selection, submission. Drop-off is visible; cause is not.
SLA
Processing volumes & SLA attainment
Daily transaction and case volumes. Percentage meeting SLA thresholds. Current backlog size. Tells you whether you are on target — not whether you will breach tomorrow.
Quality
Error & rework rates
Cases returned for correction or requiring manual intervention, by process step. Volume visible; structural cause requires Diagnostic investigation.
Adoption
Feature adoption by user segment
Which digital features are used, by which customer segments. Heat-map of engagement. Under-used features visible — reason requires user research or Diagnostic analytics.
Diagnostic Analytics · Type 2
"Why is it like this?"

When a KPI moves, Diagnostic analytics is the structured process of finding out why. It requires segmentation, attribution, and root-cause analysis — moving beyond a single number to the distribution of causes beneath it. The same headline figure can hide very different problems depending on where you look.

🏦
SME Lending — NPL Increase
Turning a single ratio into an actionable root-cause picture
Surface reading · Descriptive only
NPL ratio rose 0.4% this quarter
Single portfolio-wide number. No segmentation by sector, geography, RM, or vintage. Management response: portfolio-wide credit tightening — reduce limits, raise collateral requirements across all SME clients.
Risk: healthy sectors penalised. Good clients in stable industries lose credit access because of a problem concentrated elsewhere. Over-correction damages relationships and future NII.
Diagnostic
drill
Diagnostic segmentation
NPL is concentrated — not systemic
Segmentation reveals the structure beneath the headline number. Three distinct root causes emerge — each requiring a different response.
Response: targeted intervention in two segments only. No blanket tightening. Healthy clients untouched.
Segment Finding NPL Movement Root Cause Action
Construction (SG) Material stress +1.8% Project delays from contract disputes; cost overruns in two large accounts Sector review; enhanced monitoring; selective new origination pause
Manufacturing (MY/PH) Stable No change No systemic issue identified No change to credit terms required
Single RM — Tier-3 clients Concentration risk +2.3% 3 accounts representing 68% of RM's book; inadequate annual review cadence Supervision and account redistribution; immediate review of 3 accounts
All other segments Improving −0.2% avg Normal portfolio dynamics No intervention needed
The diagnostic principle
The same headline number (NPL +0.4%) masks four completely different situations requiring four completely different responses. Diagnostic analytics is what makes that visible.
A portfolio-wide response to a concentrated problem is almost always the wrong response — and Diagnostic work is what prevents it.

Retail Banking — Customer Attrition
Why are customers leaving — and is it one problem or several?
Surface reading · Descriptive only
Active customer count fell 3.2% this quarter
Retention rate decline noted in the monthly dashboard. Response: blanket retention offer rolled out to all at-risk customers — fee waivers and promotional deposit rates applied broadly.
Risk: costly intervention deployed to customers who were leaving for reasons unrelated to price. Fee waivers given to customers who were leaving due to poor service — they take the offer and still leave later.
Diagnostic
drill
Diagnostic segmentation
Three distinct attrition patterns — three different fixes
Exit surveys, complaint data, and journey analysis segment the attrition into causes. Each requires a different intervention type.
Targeted response by cause: rate offer to price-sensitive segment only; service escalation for service failures; digital UX fix for friction.

Digital friction lapsers 22% Drop-off at account self-service step; not completing transfers without branch visit Digital UX fix for self-service flow; in-app guidance

Predictive Analytics · Type 3
"What is likely to happen next?"

Predictive analytics uses historical patterns, statistical models, and contextual signals to produce forward-looking probability estimates. It does not eliminate uncertainty — it quantifies it, so decisions can be made with a clearer understanding of likely outcomes under different scenarios.

🏦
SME Lending — Credit Risk
Looking forward at loss probability, not just backward at NPL ratios
At Origination
Probability of Default (PD) scoring
At application, a PD model estimates likelihood of default within 12 months based on financial ratios, sector, account conduct, and bureau data. Informs credit approval decision and pricing.
Model-driven
Portfolio
Expected Credit Loss (ECL) projection
Forward-looking loss estimate under IFRS 9. Requires probability-weighted scenarios (base case, downside, severe) with macro overlays. Not a backward-looking average — a model of what loss is likely under defined conditions.
IFRS 9
Stress Testing
Macro scenario stress tests
What happens to the SME portfolio if SGD appreciates 15%? If interest rates rise 200bps? Models translate macro assumptions into sector-level impact, allowing capital adequacy forward planning.
Capital planning
Early Warning
Watch-list trigger scoring
Behavioural signals (utilisation pattern change, late payments, reduced transaction activity) are modelled into a composite early warning score. Flags accounts for proactive RM outreach before formal default.
Proactive
Where SMBC is typically strongest

Credit risk prediction is the most mature Predictive domain in most banks. PD/LGD models, IFRS 9 ECL, and stress testing frameworks are well-established. The gap for many SME teams is at the RM level — converting model scores into day-to-day portfolio management decisions, rather than using them only for origination and regulatory reporting.

👥
Retail Banking — Churn & Growth
Predicting customer behaviour before it becomes a fact in the data
Retention
Churn propensity model
Using login frequency decline, transaction volume change, complaint history, and competitor rate movements to score each customer's probability of lapsing in the next 90 days. Enables proactive outreach before exit decision is made.
90-day horizon
Cross-sell
Next-best-offer propensity scoring
For each customer, a model estimates receptivity to the next product in their financial journey — mortgage, insurance, investment — based on life stage signals, account behaviour, and cohort patterns.
Personalised
Lifetime Value
Customer LTV projection by cohort
Expected revenue contribution over the full customer relationship, projected forward from current tenure, product holdings, and segment characteristics. Informs how much retention spend is economically justified.
Investment case
Triggers
Salary credit & life event prediction
Detecting salary credit changes (promotion, job change), address changes, or family event patterns to time product offers to moments of maximum relevance — e.g., home loan at salary upgrade point.
Event-based
⚙️
Operations — Capacity & SLA
Predicting where the system will break before it does
Capacity
Volume & demand forecasting
Using seasonal patterns, product campaign calendars, and digital completion rates to forecast daily application and transaction volumes — enabling staffing decisions 1–2 weeks ahead of demand spikes.
SLA Risk
SLA breach probability model
A real-time model that scores current queue depth, processing rate, and staffing levels against historical SLA patterns. Generates a probability estimate of breach in the next 4 hours — enabling proactive diversion or escalation.
Real-time
Digital
Abandonment prediction from session signals
Within a digital application journey, models identify session-level signals (time-on-step, repeated field errors, idle time) that predict abandonment — enabling real-time intervention such as a chat prompt or callback offer.
The predictive gap in banking operations
Operations is the domain where predictive capability has the fastest ROI — but remains underdeveloped in most banks. The ability to predict a queue build 4 hours ahead of SLA breach is straightforward to model with existing data. Most teams respond after the breach instead.
The question is not whether the model can be built. It is whether the team is structured to act on its output in time.
Prescriptive Analytics · Type 4
"What should we do — and how do we refine it?"

Prescriptive analytics has two layers. The first is evidence-based policy: decisions and strategies grounded in portfolio or population data rather than convention. The second is adaptive experimentation: running controlled tests, measuring outcomes, and refining based on what actually happens — rather than implementing changes all at once and hoping.

🏦
SME Lending — Credit Policy & RM Practice
Moving from convention-based to evidence-based credit decisions
Layer 1 · Policy
Sector-specific credit criteria
Rather than applying uniform collateral and coverage ratios across all SME sectors, credit policy is calibrated to actual historical loss data by sector and vintage. Construction sector parameters differ from those for professional services based on observed performance — not convention.
Evidence-based
Layer 1 · Policy
Concentration limit calibration
Single-name and sector concentration limits derived from stress test and correlation data — not round-number approximations. Limits are reviewed when new loss correlation data emerges, not on an annual cycle only.
Data-derived
Layer 2 · Experiment
RM coaching trigger pilot
Rather than applying coaching to all RMs equally, a pilot tests whether data-triggered coaching (RM score falls below threshold → structured coaching session) outperforms calendar-based coaching. Champion group vs control group; measured on portfolio quality at 12 months.
Pilot
Layer 2 · Experiment
Sub-segment pricing trial
Rather than applying a single rate change to all SME revolving facilities, a pilot tests a new pricing structure on a matched sub-segment. Outcome measured on utilisation rate change and facility retention at 6 months before broader rollout.
Champion-challenger
The prescriptive question for credit teams
For each major credit policy in your portfolio: is this parameter set because the data tells us this is right — or because it has always been this way?
That is not a criticism. It is the diagnostic question that separates evidence-based policy from inherited convention. Both can be correct. Only one is verifiable.
👥
Retail Banking — Retention & Offers
From broadcast retention to adaptive, evidence-based customer management
Layer 1 · Policy
Tiered retention offer framework
Retention offer spend is allocated by customer LTV and churn probability score — not applied uniformly. A high-LTV, high-churn-risk customer receives a substantive retention call with a rate offer. A low-LTV account with the same churn score receives a digital nudge only.
LTV-weighted
Layer 2 · Experiment
Champion-challenger retention offers
For the high-churn-risk segment, two retention treatments are running simultaneously: a rate offer (champion) vs a service-upgrade offer (challenger). After 90 days, outcomes measured on both retention rate and margin impact. Winner deployed to full segment.
A/B test
Layer 2 · Experiment
Digital onboarding A/B test
Two versions of the digital onboarding flow — different field sequencing and identity verification steps — are tested on matched incoming cohorts. Conversion rate at each step is measured. The higher-performing flow becomes the new standard.
UX experiment
Layer 2 · Experiment
Personalised cross-sell sequencing
Instead of a single cross-sell campaign rolled out to all eligible customers simultaneously, a structured sequencing model is tested: does offering product A before product B produce higher uptake at step 2? Champion-challenger across four sequence variants.
Sequence test
⚙️
Operations — Process & Routing
Using prediction output to drive real-time operational decisions
Layer 1 · Policy
Dynamic case routing rules
When the SLA breach probability model exceeds a defined threshold, cases are automatically rerouted to a secondary processing team and an escalation flag is triggered — before the breach happens. The routing rule is derived from historical data on what intervention timing was effective.
Automated rule
Layer 2 · Experiment
Shift start-time pilot
Diagnostic analysis found Monday AM queues are 2 hours deep before the team starts. A pilot tests a staggered 7am–9am start on Monday only, with matched weeks as control. Outcome measured: SLA attainment rate and overtime cost at 8 weeks.
Controlled pilot
Layer 2 · Experiment
In-journey intervention test
Abandonment prediction model flags high-risk sessions. Pilot tests three interventions: (1) in-app chat prompt, (2) callback offer, (3) simplified field option. Conversion rate by intervention type measured over 6 weeks. The winning intervention is then integrated as standard behaviour.
3-way test
The experimentation gap
The difference between Prescriptive analytics and good intuition is that Prescriptive analytics builds in a mechanism for being wrong and learning from it. A big-bang change applied portfolio-wide cannot tell you what worked and what did not. A pilot can.
The question for your function: when you make a change, do you know whether it worked — or do you just move to the next initiative?
