Module 3B
SpaceX / Starlink IPO – Case Brief & Exercise Instructions
01
Case snapshot

What are we looking at?

SpaceX is seeking to raise approximately USD 75B at a ~USD 1.75T valuation, in what could be the largest IPO in history (ticker: SPCX, Nasdaq).
Starlink (satellite internet) is the primary economic engine, contributing the majority of 2025 revenue; launch and AI/xAI/X make up the rest.
CEO Elon Musk will retain ~85% of voting power through a dual‑class share structure (Class B super‑voting shares), leaving public investors as minority holders in a controlled company.
Context Largest IPO Controlled company
02
Facts & figures

Key numbers you will use

Business performance (FY2025): Revenue ≈ USD 18.7B, with ~30%+ growth vs 2024; adjusted EBITDA ≈ USD 6.6B (mid‑30s % margin).
Investment intensity: Capex around USD 14B in 2025, implying very high capex/revenue and negative free cash flow in the near term.
IPO pricing: Offer price USD 135/share; fully diluted shares ≈ 12.5B; implied market cap ~USD 1.7–1.8T and EV/Sales ≈ 90x, EV/EBITDA ≈ 250x versus much lower multiples for peers like Nvidia and Tesla.
Growth & margins Capex & FCF Multiples vs peers
03
Structure & market design

What’s different about this IPO?

Fast index entry: Nasdaq’s new “Fast Entry” rule allows mega‑cap IPOs like SpaceX to join the Nasdaq‑100 after just 15 trading days, rather than waiting months. Index funds and ETFs tracking the Nasdaq‑100 will therefore be forced buyers very quickly.
Float & passive flows: For qualifying large IPOs, free‑float constraints are relaxed and weight is controlled via investable‑weight factors rather than strict float thresholds, allowing low‑float mega‑caps into benchmarks while still triggering large passive flows.
Dual‑class governance: Public investors receive 1‑vote Class A shares, while Musk holds 10‑vote Class B shares and remains firmly in control; corporate governance and pension funds have flagged this as an investor‑unfriendly structure.
Fast‑track Nasdaq‑100 Passive ETF demand Dual‑class control
04
Your assignment

What you need to deliver

1
Valuation view. Using the facts above and the Excel data pack, form an initial view: at USD 135/share, is SPCX cheap, fair, or expensive relative to its growth, margins and peers? You may sketch a simple DCF or rely on multiples, but your answer must be anchored in numbers.
2
Risk mapping. Identify at least three material risks across: (a) business/financial (growth, capex, FCF), (b) governance (dual‑class, Musk control), and (c) market structure (fast index entry, float, ETF flows).
3
Recommendation. Decide: GO / CAUTION / NO‑GO for a long‑term institutional investor at the IPO price. Support your view with three concrete data points: at least one from the financials, one from the IPO structure/rules, and one from your valuation work.

Work in small groups. Allocate ~10 minutes to crunch numbers in Excel, ~10 minutes to debate risks and structure, and ~5 minutes to agree your one‑line recommendation.
