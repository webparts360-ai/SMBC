The Evolution of Decision Making

Every generation of analytical tools improved the information available to managers — but the gap between having data and making a decision remained. Understanding where we are now explains why the frameworks in this module matter.

📊
Mentimeter · Word Cloud
"In one word — how does your team currently make its most important decisions?"
1
1950s – 70s
Expert Systems

Rules-based logic and codified human expertise. Decisions were only as good as the rules programmed into the system.

2
1980s – 90s
Econometrics & Statistics

Regression models, forecasting, and spreadsheet-based scenario planning. The first era where structured data routinely informed strategic choices.

3
2000s
Business Intelligence

Dashboards, data warehouses, and OLAP reporting. Data became visible across organisations — but mostly in arrears.

4
2010s
Big Data & Advanced Analytics

Machine learning, predictive models, and unstructured data at scale. Analysis moved from describing the past to anticipating the future.

★
Now
Generative AI & Copilot

Natural language queries against your own data. AI-assisted insight and action. The gap between asking a question and getting an answer is collapsing — but the quality of the answer still depends on the quality of the question.

Why This Is Now a Strategic Imperative

The performance gap between data-led and intuition-led organisations is not marginal — it is structural. As a VP or ED, your role is not to build analytical models. It is to ensure your organisation uses this capability and that your decisions are informed by it.

Key Principle

Data analytics is no longer a support function. It is a source of competitive advantage. The question is not whether to use it — it is whether you are using it faster and more effectively than your competitors.

How Analytics Supports Strategic Planning

Analytics is not a separate process that happens in the data team. It should run through every phase of the strategic planning cycle. The question is not "should we use data?" — it is "what data do we need at each stage, and do we have it?"

Set Direction
◈ Annual Planning

Analyse market size, growth rates, and segment profitability. Benchmark performance against competitors. Identify underserved customer segments through transaction data.

Allocate Resources
◉ Budgeting

Model ROI scenarios across product lines, markets, or channels. Track resource utilisation against planned targets. Identify where investment is generating versus destroying value.

Monitor & Adapt
◎ Performance Reviews

Build leading indicators that signal performance shifts early. Track strategic KPIs — not just operational metrics. Close the loop: compare actual outcomes to planned assumptions.

Key Principle
The Boundary

Analytics does not replace strategic judgement. It reduces the uncertainty around it. The DataPOV framework — covered next — is the bridge between the data and the decision.

The Observation Trap

Most data presentations fail to drive decisions — not because the data is wrong, but because they stop at observation. An observation tells you what happened. A recommendation tells you what to do about it.

📊
Mentimeter · Multiple Choice
"How is data typically presented to you?" A) Summary of what happened   B) Recommendation with evidence   C) A dashboard I interpret myself   D) Rarely — mostly verbal
Observation
What most people produce
  • Loan uptake has declined 18% over 3 quarters
  • Deposit balances in the same segment have grown 12%
  • A competitor launched a digital application 8 weeks ago
  • Our application process takes 14 minutes vs. competitor's 4
This is a summary of evidence. It is useful — but it is not a recommendation. Nothing in this list tells anyone what to do.
Recommendation
What decisions require
  • A clear point of view — not just the data
  • A specific action — a verb that commits to a direction
  • The stakes — what is gained or lost either way
  • Confidence to own it — even with incomplete data
This is what a DataPOV produces. The same evidence, but interpreted — with a recommended course of action and the consequences named explicitly.

The Anatomy of a DataPOV

A DataPOV is a single, complete sentence with three components. All three are required. Without the specific action it is an observation. Without the stakes it is a weak recommendation.

01
The Data Observation
[What the data shows]
State the evidence — not your interpretation of it
Good

Loan uptake in the 35–50 segment has declined 18% over 3 consecutive quarters despite deposit balance growth of 12% in the same cohort.

Weak

Our loan product is underperforming.

The good version states measurable facts. The weak version states a conclusion masquerading as evidence.

02
The Specific Action
means we must [verb + direction]
A strong verb that commits to a course of action
Good

…means we must immediately audit our digital application journey against competitor benchmarks and present a remediation roadmap to the product committee within 30 days…

Weak

…means we must review our loan product strategy.

"Audit and present within 30 days" is an action. "Review strategy" is a meeting agenda item.

03
The Stakes
otherwise [what is gained or lost]
Name what changes — in either direction
Good

…or risk permanently losing deposit-relationship customers to a competitor who has already removed the friction we have not addressed.

Weak

…or our performance may continue to decline.

The good version names a specific, irreversible consequence. The weak version is a truism that adds no urgency.

The DataPOV Formula
[What the data shows] means we must [specific action], otherwise [what is at stake].
"Loan uptake in the 35–50 segment has declined 18% despite deposit growth, and service data rules out quality as the cause — means we must benchmark and remediate our digital application process within 30 days, or risk permanently losing our highest-value deposit relationships to a faster competitor."

The Verb Is the Decision

The action verb is the most important word in a DataPOV sentence. Weak verbs do not protect you from being wrong — they delay the decision. Strong verbs make the recommendation visible, which is what good leadership requires.

Weak Verb Strong Verb Why It Matters
Review→ AuditImplies investigation is needed — not that it has happened
Consider→ PrioritiseNames the trade-off explicitly
Explore→ PilotCommits to a bounded test with a timeline
Monitor→ EscalateSignals the threshold for action has been reached
Assess→ RecommendTakes ownership of the conclusion
Look into→ CommissionAssigns accountability and resources
Discuss→ Decide by [date]Closes the loop on the conversation
Improve→ Reduce X by Y%Makes the outcome measurable
A Note on Context

Banking organisations tend toward cautious language in written communication. This is appropriate for board papers and external communication. The DataPOV is an internal thinking discipline — the strong verb gives you the clarity you need before you soften the language for your audience.

Building a DataPOV — Step by Step

Here is how the three components are assembled from the same set of raw data points. Notice that the observation uses two contrasting data points — the decline and the absence of service complaints — to rule out an alternative explanation before stating the conclusion.

The Raw Data

  • Loan applications, 35–50 segment: ↓18% over 3 quarters
  • Deposit balance, same segment: ↑12% same period
  • Loan complaint volume: flat — not service-related
  • Competitor digital app launched: 8 weeks ago
  • Our application time vs. competitor: 14 min vs. ~4 min
01
Start with the observation

Loan uptake in the 35–50 segment has declined 18% over 3 quarters despite deposit balance growth in the same cohort — and complaint data rules out service quality as the cause.

02
Add the specific action

…means we must immediately benchmark our digital application journey against the competitor's process and present a remediation roadmap to the product committee within 30 days…

03
Add the stakes

…or risk permanently losing our most valuable deposit-relationship segment to a competitor who has already removed the friction we have not addressed.

✓
The complete DataPOV

Loan uptake in the 35–50 segment has declined 18% despite deposit growth, and service data rules out quality as the cause — means we must benchmark and remediate our digital application process within 30 days, or risk permanently losing our highest-value deposit relationships to a faster competitor.

Debrief Guide

After writing, responses are revealed simultaneously in Mentimeter. Use these questions to guide 10 minutes of discussion:

  • Find two sentences that recommend the same action — did they use the same data to get there?
  • Find two sentences that recommend opposite actions — what drove the difference?
  • Which sentences have the strongest stakes? What makes them feel urgent?
  • Where do you see weak verbs? What would you replace them with?

Close with: "The tools you will use tomorrow exist to build the evidence base for the sentence you just wrote. The DataPOV comes first — the tool helps you prove it."
