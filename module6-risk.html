Traditional risk management is structured around time — monthly reviews, quarterly reports, annual stress tests. Analytics replaces the calendar with signals. This session shows what that shift means in practice, and what it demands of you as a manager.

Part 1 From periodic review to continuous monitoring — what changes

The most important thing analytics does to risk management is not make it more accurate. It makes it faster. The traditional cycle — collect data, compile report, review at scheduled meeting, decide — introduces weeks or months of lag between when a risk signal appears and when a manager sees it. In credit, compliance, and operations, that lag has a cost. The visual below maps what changes.

Traditional model
Periodic review — structured around the calendar
↻ Risk is assessed at fixed intervals — monthly credit reviews, quarterly compliance reports, annual stress tests
↻ Data is aggregated before review — individual signals are averaged or summarised before a manager sees them
↻ Action depends on the next scheduled meeting — a risk identified on day 3 of the month may not surface until day 28
↻ The manager's role is to review — read the report, validate the findings, approve or escalate
→
Analytics-enabled model
Continuous monitoring — structured around thresholds
✓ Risk signals are monitored in real time or near-real time — the system flags when a threshold is crossed, not when the calendar says to look
✓ Individual borrower or transaction signals surface directly — patterns are visible before they aggregate into portfolio-level problems
✓ Action is triggered by the signal, not the schedule — response time is measured in days or hours, not weeks
✓ The manager's role is to respond — know what the signal means, know what the decision is, act within the threshold window
The managerial implication
Continuous monitoring only works if the manager on the receiving end knows what to do with a signal. A threshold breach that lands in an inbox and generates no response is no better than a monthly report that goes unread. The system changes the speed. The manager's analytical capability determines whether that speed advantage is used.
This is the same failure as the mortgage bank — except the warning now arrives in real time. The question is the same: when the signal lands, what do you do with it?
!
APAC regulatory context
Continuous monitoring is increasingly a regulatory expectation, not a best practice
MAS (Singapore) has issued guidance under MAS 626 and the AML/CFT framework requiring banks to implement ongoing transaction monitoring with alert-based escalation — not just periodic sampling.
HKMA (Hong Kong) expects real-time or near-real-time transaction monitoring as part of its Supervisory Policy Manual AML-2 standards. Periodic batch review is increasingly viewed as insufficient for higher-risk customer segments.
Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas and Bank Negara Malaysia have both signalled expectations that AML controls reflect the volume and velocity of digital transaction flows — which batch processing cannot keep pace with.
1

Credit risk. Continuous monitoring surfaces borrower deterioration — rising utilisation, payment pattern changes, sector stress exposure — weeks before a formal review cycle would catch it. The question is whether your team is commissioning forward-looking signals or backward-looking confirmation.

2

Transaction monitoring. Pattern-based analytics flags anomalies in real time — unusual velocity, structuring behaviour, counterparty changes — rather than surfacing them in a monthly reconciliation. The value is in lead time. Lead time requires a manager who knows what the alert means.

3

Compliance monitoring. AML, KYC, and conduct obligations can be monitored continuously against defined thresholds — expiry clustering, client review backlogs, policy breach rates. Continuous monitoring converts compliance from a periodic audit function into a live operational control.

The managerial question this raises

In your current credit review process, what is the realistic lag between a borrower exhibiting the first signals of stress and a manager seeing it as a classified risk? If the answer is measured in months, the question is not whether continuous monitoring would add value. It is whether you have the analytical infrastructure — and the management capability — to act on it when it arrives.

Part 3 Signal-to-action reference — three domains, APAC context

This reference maps specific risk signals — by domain — to the data source that surfaces them, the threshold that should trigger management attention, and the decision the manager needs to make. Use it as a starting point to map your own area: which signals does your team currently monitor, which are missing, and which have no defined management response?

Risk signals — credit, transaction & compliance
Domain Signal Data source Threshold for action Management decision Severity
Credit Revolving facility utilisation spike Core banking system — daily draw-down data Utilisation exceeds 85% for 5+ consecutive days, or jumps >20% in a single week Initiate unscheduled relationship call. Review cash flow position. Assess whether drawdown reflects operational need or liquidity stress. High
Credit Payment pattern deterioration Loan servicing system — payment date and amount tracking First occurrence of 15-DPD on any facility; or trend of payments arriving progressively later over 3 consecutive months Flag for watch-list review. Do not approve new drawdowns pending assessment. Escalate to credit committee if 30-DPD reached. High
Credit Sector stress correlation External market data feed — sector credit indices, PMI, trade flow data Borrower's sector PMI falls below 48 for 2 consecutive months AND borrower has >60% revenue from that sector Commission sector stress scenario analysis for affected exposures. Review concentration limits. Consider whether new originations in sector require enhanced credit conditions. Medium
Credit Covenant proximity alert Financial covenant monitoring system — quarterly financials input Borrower within 10% of any financial covenant threshold (DSCR, leverage, interest cover) Proactive engagement before breach. Review waiver position and escalation protocol. Assess whether covenant reset is appropriate or whether exposure reduction is required. High
Credit Collateral value erosion Property valuation feeds, equity price monitoring — automated revaluation LTV rises above 80% due to collateral value decline of >10% from last formal valuation Instruct independent revaluation. Review margin call or additional security requirements per facility agreement. Escalate if LTV breach confirmed. High
Transaction Unusual transaction velocity Transaction monitoring system — real-time or T+1 feeds Transaction volume exceeds 3x the 90-day average in a 48-hour window, particularly in cash or near-cash instruments Generate SAR consideration report. Relationship manager to contact client for business justification. Do not alert client to monitoring activity. Escalate to Compliance within 24 hours. High
Transaction Structuring pattern detection Transaction monitoring system — pattern recognition model Multiple transactions just below reporting thresholds (e.g., <SGD 20,000) from same originator within 72 hours Immediate escalation to Compliance / MLRO. Do not process further transactions pending review. Follow MAS Notice 626 / HKMA AML-2 escalation procedures. High
Transaction Counterparty jurisdiction change Payment processing system — beneficiary country flagging New counterparty in FATF high-risk or monitored jurisdiction, or sudden shift away from established counterparty network Enhanced due diligence on new counterparty. Hold transaction pending compliance sign-off for sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions. Document rationale. High
Transaction Round-sum transaction clustering Transaction monitoring system — round-number detection module Three or more transactions of identical or near-identical round amounts (e.g., exactly USD 50,000) within 30 days to/from same counterparty Review transaction purpose and business justification with relationship manager. Assess against customer risk profile. Escalate if no credible explanation obtained within 48 hours. Medium
Compliance KYC renewal expiry clustering Client lifecycle management system — expiry date tracking dashboard More than 15% of a portfolio segment's KYC reviews falling due in a single 30-day window, or any high-risk client review overdue by >7 days Redistribute review workload across team. Escalate high-risk client overdue cases immediately to Compliance. Do not approve new transactions for clients with overdue reviews per MAS 626. High
Compliance Policy breach rate trend Internal audit and compliance tracking system — exception log Policy exceptions exceed 5% of transactions in any team for two consecutive months, or any single exception type exceeds 3% alone Root cause analysis of exception pattern. Determine whether breach reflects a process failure, training gap, or policy ambiguity. Report to Risk Committee with remediation plan within 30 days. Medium
Compliance Adverse media — client mention Automated adverse media screening tool — daily sweep of news and regulatory databases Any mention of existing client in regulatory enforcement action, criminal proceedings, or significant reputational event Immediate notification to Compliance and relationship senior management. Assess whether relationship continuation is appropriate. Review for SAR obligation. Do not discuss with client pending internal decision. High
Compliance Sanctions list match — new designation Sanctions screening system — automated feed from OFAC, UN, MAS, HKMA lists Any exact or fuzzy match (>85% confidence) between existing client or counterparty and updated sanctions designation Freeze all transactions immediately. Notify Compliance and Legal within 1 hour. No client contact until legal position confirmed. Follow jurisdictional reporting obligations (MAS, HKMA, AUSTRAC) within prescribed timeframe. High
How to use this reference in your team

The gaps in those three columns are your action items.
