Banker's Cheat Sheet

Financial Metrics
in DataPOV language

Each metric is translated into a banker-friendly statement using this structure: what the data shows, what action we must take, and what is at stake if we ignore it.

Format DataPOV reference statements
Audience Bankers and credit teams
Use Training, facilitation, discussion
Reference Metrics rewritten into action-and-stakes language
01
Valuation
P/E Ratio
The market is pricing this company at 10 years of current earnings, which means we must test whether earnings are sustainable, otherwise we may overpay for profits that do not materialise.
02
Profitability
Net Margin
Only RM8 out of every RM100 of sales becomes net profit, which means we must examine cost control and earnings resilience, otherwise small shocks may erase profitability.
03
Profitability
Gross Margin
The business keeps RM30 out of every RM100 before overhead and financing costs, which means we must judge whether pricing power is strong enough, otherwise rising costs may quickly compress profit.
04
Return
ROA
Every RM100 invested in assets generates RM5 of profit, which means we must assess whether the asset base is productive enough, otherwise capital may remain tied up in underperforming assets.
05
Return
ROE
Shareholders are earning RM20 a year for every RM100 of equity, which means we must check whether returns come from genuine performance or excessive leverage, otherwise the apparent strength may hide elevated risk.
06
Liquidity
Current Ratio
The company has RM1.50 of near-term assets for every RM1 due within a year, which means we must test how liquid those assets really are, otherwise a paper buffer may still turn into a cash squeeze.
07
Liquidity
Quick Ratio
Without relying on inventory, only 75% of short-term obligations are covered, which means we must assess immediate liquidity and refinancing options, otherwise the borrower may struggle to meet near-term payments.
08
Leverage
Debt-to-Equity
The business is using RM2 of debt for every RM1 of equity, which means we must evaluate leverage tolerance and sponsor support, otherwise lenders may be carrying too much of the downside risk.
09
Coverage
Interest Coverage
Operating profit covers the interest bill four times, which means we must stress-test earnings under weaker conditions, otherwise a moderate profit drop could threaten debt servicing capacity.
10
Working Capital
Cash Conversion Cycle
Cash is tied up for about 60 days between paying suppliers and collecting from customers, which means we must plan funding for that operating gap, otherwise growth may create liquidity strain instead of strength.
DataPOV Formula
Standard syntax
Template
[What the data shows] means we must [specific action], otherwise [what is at stake].

How How to speak in DataPOV language
1

State the number in plain English first, not as a formula.

2

Follow immediately with the action bankers must take.

3

End with the risk if no action is taken.

Example

“Operating profit covers interest only 2 times, which means we must stress-test earnings resilience, otherwise a modest decline could impair debt servicing.”

Facilitator Note
Why this works
Evidence What the number says
Decision What must happen next
Consequence Why it matters now
Guide Use judgment, not just the number
!
Interpretation Reminder
A strong ratio can still mislead
A good-looking ratio may be driven by temporary earnings, overstated assets, slow-moving inventory, weak receivables quality, or leverage that is not immediately obvious.
