Audience Bankers, credit teams, facilitators

Core Impact-oriented meanings for common financial numbers
01
Valuation

P/E Ratio

How many years of current earnings you are paying for when you buy the share today.

Banker translation Price = pre-paid future profits
Watch-out Only meaningful if earnings are sustainable
Example
Share price 60 / EPS 6 = P/E of 10x
Meaning: at today's earnings level, it takes about 10 years to recover the purchase price.
02
Profitability

Net Margin

Out of every 1 ringgit of sales, how many sen end up as true bottom-line profit.

Example
Net profit 8m / Revenue 100m = 8%
Meaning: every RM100 sold leaves RM8 after all costs, interest, and tax.
03
Efficiency

Gross Margin

How much pricing cushion remains after paying the direct cost of goods or services delivered.

Example
Revenue 100m - COGS 70m = 30m gross profit; 30m / 100m = 30%
Meaning: the business keeps RM30 out of every RM100 before overheads and financing costs.
04
Return

ROA

How hard the total asset base is working to generate profit.

Example
Net income 5m / Total assets 100m = 5%
Meaning: every RM100 invested in assets produces RM5 of annual profit.
05
Return

ROE

How much the owners earn each year on the capital they actually have at risk.

Example
Net income 5m / Equity 25m = 20%
Meaning: shareholders earn RM20 a year for every RM100 of equity left inside the business.
06
Liquidity

Current Ratio

Whether near-term assets appear sufficient to cover obligations due within 12 months.

Example
Current assets 24m / Current liabilities 16m = 1.5x
Meaning: the business has RM1.50 of near-term resources for every RM1 due soon.
07
Liquidity

Quick Ratio

The same liquidity test, but excluding inventory that may not turn into cash quickly.

Example
(Cash 4m + Receivables 8m) / Current liabilities 16m = 0.75x
Meaning: without selling stock, only 75% of short-term obligations are covered.
08
Leverage

Debt-to-Equity

How much borrowed money supports the business relative to the owners' money.

Example
Total debt 60m / Equity 30m = 2.0x
Meaning: lenders have put in RM2 for every RM1 left by the owners.
09
Coverage

Interest Coverage

How many times operating profit can pay the annual interest bill before lenders get nervous.

Example
EBIT 12m / Interest expense 3m = 4.0x
Meaning: operating profit can cover the interest bill four times over.
10
Working capital

Cash Conversion Cycle

How many days cash is tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers.

Example
Inventory 50 days + Receivables 40 days - Payables 30 days = 60 days
Meaning: the business must fund about 60 days of operations before cash comes back in.

Use How to explain ratios so bankers act on them
1

Start with the business decision, not the formula. Example: “Can this borrower service debt comfortably?”

2

Translate each ratio into a time, cash, or risk statement bankers can visualize immediately.

3

Pair the ratio with a threshold or comparison, such as trend, peer, covenant, or prior year.

4

Always ask what can distort it: one-off profit, stale inventory, inflated receivables, or revaluation gains.

A
Conversation format

A pragmatic script

Template
“The number is X. In plain terms, that means Y. For a banker, the real question is Z.”
Example: “Interest coverage is 4x. In plain terms, current operating profit can pay interest four times. For a banker, the question is whether this still holds if profit drops 25%.”
Best practice
Make it visual
Time ratiosExplain in years or days
Margin ratiosExplain in sen per RM1
Leverage ratiosExplain whose money is funding the business
Guide Interpret numbers with judgment, not in isolation
!
Interpretation

Common traps

A “good” number can still hide weak credit quality if the underlying earnings, assets, or working capital are not real or not repeatable.

High ROECould be boosted by excessive leverage
Strong current ratioCould be padded by slow inventory
Low P/ECould signal falling earnings ahead
Good marginCould be inflated by one-off gains
