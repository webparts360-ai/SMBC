You are a member of SMBC's internal investment steering committee. Three of the largest AI companies in the world are approaching the public markets. Your committee must form a position before the board presentation. The data is in front of you. The decision is yours.

Context

AI companies have attracted more capital in the last three years than any sector in history. Three of them are now moving toward public markets. SMBC — as a major institutional bank — will be asked to take a view: as an equity participant, as a lender, and as a risk manager. This exercise puts you in that seat.

The Central Question
Given this data, what is SMBC's position on each issuer — and how do you defend it with numbers?
Your answer must reference at least two data points per issuer. Assertion without evidence is not a committee position.

Learning Objectives

1

Distinguish between confirmed financial data, analyst estimates, and speculative projections in an investment briefing pack.

2

Apply EV/Revenue and burn-rate analysis to evaluate valuation credibility under different scenarios.

3

Articulate a risk-adjusted position — equity, credit, or pass — using structured analytical reasoning rather than intuition.

4

Recognise that the same data supports multiple defensible conclusions, and that the quality of reasoning matters more than the conclusion reached.

30 Minutes Committee Tasks

Your breakout group plays three roles simultaneously. Each role approaches the same issuers from a different risk and return mandate. Complete Task 1 together, then split into roles for Tasks 2 and 3, then reconvene to form a single committee position.

5 min Task 1: Data triage
10 min Task 2: Valuation
10 min Task 3: Role positions
5 min Debrief prep
01
All Roles · 5 min
Data Triage — Classify Before You Conclude

Before any analysis, the committee must agree on what the data actually says. Review the Data Pack and for each issuer, classify the two most important figures you would rely on — and flag the one number you are most sceptical about.

  • A
    Identify two figures per issuer you would rely on

    What has a credible source — a formal filing, a confirmed funding round, a named company statement? These are your anchor points.

  • B
    Flag one number you would not use without further diligence

    Look for secondary market valuations, analyst projections, or figures sourced from unnamed investor analyses. Label them accordingly.

  • C
    Note: Starlink X is a proposed entity, not a completed corporate structure

    The term "Starlink X" appears in analyst notes and press reports. There is no formal filing or agreed governance structure as of mid-2026. What does that mean for any analysis you do on it?

02
All Roles · 10 min
Valuation Sanity Check

Use Table 1 and Table 4 from the Data Pack to work through these calculations as a group.

  • A
    Calculate EV/Revenue for Anthropic and OpenAI

    Use the primary round valuations (not secondary market figures) and the revenue figures in Table 1. Compare your results to the Microsoft and Nvidia benchmarks in Table 4. What premium are investors paying, and why might that be justified — or not?

  • B
    Stress-test OpenAI under two scenarios

    Scenario A: Microsoft reprices its compute arrangement, raising OpenAI's cost of revenue by 20%. If gross margin falls accordingly, what does the P&L look like?
    Scenario B: Revenue growth stalls at $13B for two years while losses continue at the current run rate. How much additional capital is required before 2028?

  • C
    Consider the 3% ROI figure from Table 5

    Only 3% of enterprises report 10%+ ROI on AI deployments. If this is the current state of enterprise AI adoption, what does it imply for the revenue trajectory assumptions baked into a $852B valuation?

03
By Role · 10 min
Form Your Committee Position

Each role takes a different lens. Answer your role questions, then come back together and present a single unified committee recommendation per issuer: Back / Conditional / Pass.

Role A
Equity Syndicate Desk
  • Would you participate in this IPO? At what maximum EV/Revenue multiple?
  • Which issuer has the strongest revenue growth story relative to its valuation?
  • What single condition would need to change for you to upgrade or downgrade your view?
Role B
Credit & Lending Desk
  • Would you extend a term loan to any of these issuers? Under what covenants?
  • Which issuer poses the highest debt-service risk given burn rate and margins?
  • What collateral or structural protection would you require?
Role C
Risk & Compliance
  • Which issuer carries the highest reputational risk for SMBC as a backer?
  • Starlink's privacy policy change and OpenAI's litigation exposure: how do you price these?
  • What conditions or exclusions would you attach before SMBC takes any exposure?
Deliverable
One committee position per issuer: Back / Conditional / Pass — with a one-sentence rationale supported by at least two data points.
Example: "Conditional on Anthropic — 9× EV/Revenue is defensible at 40% gross margin, but inference cost trajectory (+23%) and burn rate signal require a margin covenant in any debt structure."

Facilitator Debrief Guide
Facilitator Note

This panel is for facilitator use during the 10-minute plenary debrief. The goal is not to reach a consensus answer — it is to surface the quality of reasoning and to make the data literacy lesson explicit. Different groups will reach different positions; that is the intended outcome.

What to Surface in Debrief

  • 1
    Did groups use data flags correctly?

    Ask: "Which numbers did you rely on most heavily? Were they confirmed, estimated, or speculative?" If groups relied on secondary market valuations or the $19T GDP projection without flagging them, use this to make the data literacy point: the quality of a decision is bounded by the quality of the data inputs.

  • 2
    Did different groups reach different conclusions from the same data?

    If they did — excellent. Ask why. The answer will be: different weights assigned to different data points; different assumptions about future revenue growth; different risk mandates by role. This is the central lesson. Data informs decisions; it does not make them.

  • 3
    Probe the OpenAI EV/Revenue calculation

    65× EV/Revenue against a pre-profit company with $44B of projected losses. Ask: "What has to be true for that valuation to be right?" The answer requires an assumption about future revenue trajectory. That assumption is not in the data — it is a judgement call. Where does that judgement come from, and how do you test it?

  • 4
    Connect to their actual roles

    Close with: "You will see AI companies approaching SMBC for debt facilities, custody arrangements, and investment banking mandates in the next 24 months. The skills used in this exercise — classifying data quality, calculating valuation ratios, stress-testing under scenarios, and forming a risk-adjusted position — are the same skills you will need in that conversation."

The Core Learning

The exercise has no correct answer. A well-reasoned Pass is as valuable as a well-reasoned Back. What the exercise tests is whether participants can distinguish between data that is confirmed, estimated, and speculative — and whether they can form a structured analytical position rather than an instinctive one. That is what data-driven decision making looks like at the VP and ED level.

Scoring Rubric (Optional)

If you wish to provide structured feedback to groups, use this rubric. It is not competitive — it is a framework for self-assessment.

Dimension Strong Developing
Data classification Distinguishes confirmed from estimated from speculative; flags Starlink X as unformed entity Treats all figures at face value; does not interrogate source or scope
Quantitative reasoning Calculates EV/Revenue correctly; uses benchmark comparators; runs at least one scenario Relies on qualitative description of figures without calculation
Role differentiation Equity, credit, and risk positions are distinct and reflect different mandates All three roles reach the same conclusion by the same logic
Structured position Back/Conditional/Pass is supported by explicit data references and a stated assumption Position is stated without evidence or relies on a single data point
Data literacy Identifies at least one figure they would not use without further diligence, and explains why Does not distinguish between high- and low-confidence data
