Six principles from Storytelling with Data applied to the dashboards and reports you review and commission every day.

Six lessons The core framework — in sequence
01
Understand context first
Who is the audience? What decision must they make? Data is evidence for a story — it is not the starting point.
02
Choose the right visual
Lines for trends, bars for comparisons, tables when exact values matter. Avoid pie charts and 3D — they obscure magnitude.
03
Eliminate clutter
Every element added costs cognitive load. Remove gridlines, borders, and labels that do not directly serve the story.
04
Direct attention
Use colour, size, and position intentionally. The audience's eye should land on the insight first — not wander across the page.
05
Form follows function
Design around what you need the audience to do with the data — not around what looks impressive in the report.
06
Tell one story
A chart title states the finding, not the topic. "NPL ratio rising in Q3" beats "NPL ratio by quarter" every time.
The core question — ask before every chart
Who needs to know what, and what should they do with it?
If you cannot answer this in one sentence, the chart should not exist in the report.

Before & after — chart type and clutter

Before — common mistakes
Loan portfolio: Corporate 42%, Retail 21%, SME 14%, Trade Finance 10%, Property 8%, Other 5%.

Six competing colours · legend separated from data · 3D distorts proportions · title names the topic, not the finding · audience must calculate mentally

After — storytelling with data
Corporate loans 42% highlighted; Retail 21%, SME 14%, Trade Finance 10%, Property 8%, Other 5% in grey.

Single accent on the key finding · grey for context · title states the insight · no legend needed · bars start at zero

Before & after — trend communication

Before — spaghetti chart
Six product lines plotted with equal weight — no visual hierarchy, impossible to isolate any trend.

All lines equal weight · competing colours · audience cannot identify which line warrants attention or action

After — focus on what matters
Trade finance highlighted in gold, rising steeply. Five other lines greyed out as context.

Key line highlighted in gold · others retained as context in grey · direct label replaces legend · story is immediate

Apply now Quick rules for managers reviewing dashboards
Ask: what decision does this drive?
If no one can answer in one sentence, the chart should not be in the report. Data without a decision is decoration.
The squint test
Squint at the chart. What you still see is what your audience will remember. If it is not the key finding, something is wrong.
Colour encodes meaning, not decoration
If everything is highlighted, nothing is. Reserve the accent colour for the single insight that requires action.
Bar charts must start at zero
A truncated y-axis exaggerates differences. Always check where the axis begins before accepting a chart as evidence.
Titles state the finding
Rename every chart from a topic to a finding. "Monthly revenue" → "Revenue fell 12% in October." The title is the story.
Remove before you add
Before requesting a new visual element, ask what existing element could be removed to make the same point more clearly.
