DataPOV Exercise

A 20-minute practical close to Day 1 that bridges conceptual understanding to the tool work on Day 2.

5
Setup
5
Individual writing
10
Group debrief
20
Total
1
Setup
5 min

Share the three scenario cards on screen. Ask participants to choose the scenario closest to their function — Retail/Product, Credit Risk, or Operations. Introduce the DataPOV structure before writing begins.

The DataPOV Formula
"[What the data shows] means we must [specific action], otherwise [what is at stake]."
e.g. "Declining loan uptake despite growing deposits in our core segment means we must urgently review our application process against the competitive standard, or risk permanently losing a high-value product relationship with customers who already trust us."

Emphasise: one sentence. A complete recommendation — not a summary of the data.

2
Individual Writing
5 min

Participants write individually — camera on, no discussion. Use Mentimeter's open text feature or hold Webex chat submissions until time is called. Resist the urge to fill the silence.

3
Group Debrief
10 min

Invite 3–4 volunteers to read their sentence aloud, then facilitate discussion:

  • Where do people land on the same action — and where do they diverge?
  • What additional data would change your view or increase your confidence?
  • How easy was it to commit to a single, specific action?

Close with: "The tools we cover tomorrow — Excel, Power BI — exist to help you build the data case that supports a statement like the one you just wrote. The DataPOV comes first; the tool helps you prove it."

Facilitator Note

The most valuable debrief moments occur when two participants write opposite recommendations from the same data. This is not a problem — it is the point. Surface it explicitly: the same data supports different conclusions depending on risk appetite and strategic context. Use it as a segue into why structured data communication matters.

Module 3 — Concept Framing

From Data to Decision

How to introduce the DataPOV concept to participants before the activity.

Most managers receive data every day — dashboards, reports, performance packs. But receiving data and acting on data are two different skills. The gap is not analytical. It is communicative. The question is not "what does the data show?" It is "what does this mean we should do — and what is at stake if we don't?"

Level 1
Discrete
A single dip into a dataset to answer a specific question. One data point validates stopping, starting, or continuing.
Should we renew this contract? Did the campaign lift conversion?
Level 2
Operational
An ongoing feed of performance data. Dashboards track whether metrics move as expected or whether anomalies warrant investigation.
Are loan arrears within tolerance? Where is SLA performance slipping?
Your primary domain
Level 3
Strategic
Synthesising information from multiple sources to determine organisational direction. Requires a point of view, not just a summary.
Should we enter this segment? How do we respond to a competitor's product move?

The DataPOV

At the strategic level, data does not speak for itself. A DataPOV is a single, complete sentence capturing what the data tells you and what must be done about it. It has two parts: a specific action (a verb with direction) and the stakes (what happens either way).

DataPOV Structure
[What the data shows] means we must [specific action], otherwise [what is at stake].
The action verb is the most important word. Vague verbs — "review", "consider", "explore" — signal the point of view is not yet formed. Strong verbs — "accelerate", "exit", "consolidate", "prioritise" — signal a real recommendation.

As a VP or ED you are a commissioner and interpreter of analysis. The DataPOV is the output your team should bring to you, and the standard you should hold yourself to when escalating upward.

Your SEA retail portfolio shows sustained decline in personal loan uptake among the 35–50 segment over three quarters — your highest average deposit balance cohort. Competitor activity has increased in the same period.

Loan applications 35–50 segment: ↓18% over 3 quarters
Average deposit balance, same segment: ↑12% same period
Loan complaint volume: flat (not a service issue)
Competitor digital loan app launched: 8 weeks ago
Our application time vs. competitor: 14 min vs. ~4 min
What does this mean you must do — and what is at stake?
